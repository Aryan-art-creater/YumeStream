A Silent Voice (Japanese: 聲の形, Hepburn: Koe no Katachi) is a 2016 Japanese animated psychological drama film directed by Naoko Yamada and produced by Kyoto Animation, based on the manga of the same name by Yoshitoki Ōima. The film follows Shōya Ishida, a high school boy who, as a child, led a group in bullying Shōko Nishimiya, a deaf girl who transferred into his elementary school. Despite Shōko's attempts to communicate through a notebook, Shōya and his classmates torment her, including stealing her hearing aids and mocking her disability, leading to her transfer to another school. When the principal discovers the bullying, Shōya is made the sole scapegoat, resulting in his social isolation and ostracization throughout middle and high school. Years later, as a depressed and lonely high school student, Shōya contemplates suicide but decides to seek redemption by reconnecting with Shōko. He finds her and begins to rebuild their relationship, learning sign language to better communicate with her.
